The Monsters We Make: Murder, Obsession, and the Rise of Criminal Profiling - 7/10

I'm always happy when I find myself enjoying a random pick from the library shelves. As the first nonfiction/essay type book I've reviewed here (as well as the first I've read in a while), take all of this with a grain of salt, as I'm not as well versed in this genre. It did feel a lot like Youtube video essays I've seen in the past (and I would definitely recommend if you're a previous breadtube enjoyer). 

The book touches on many different subjects, from serial killers and criminal psychology to the CIA's brainwashing experiments and police harrassment, weaving together a web of topics that allows a glimpse into how people want to view criminals in certain ways to fit their views. I don't think I could explain the theme of the book very well but I found the material thoroughly interesting. The narrator takes a more detached, informative perspective for most of the book, other than in the small (but fascinating) personal anecdotes she shares that underline what stakes she has in this topic and her own desires to understand how and why we choose to view "evil" people the way we do.

In summary, I recommend the book. Will maybe look for more nonfiction in the future.

Wintersmith - 7/10

I enjoyed this one a lot, definitely my favorite child-friendly/child appropriate book I've read this summer. It feels very whimsical and joyful and while it does have an overarching plot around the "big bad" it also spends a lot of time building up their world, Tiffany's relationships, and the intricacies of witch politics and customs. The world feels very alive and interconnected and fantastical in a way that I miss from my childhood, and it doesn't feel like it talks down to its reader. Definitely recommend! It's the third chronological book in the Tiffany Aching Saga though, so be forewarned.

I also knocked down my rating for Tiffany Aching's Guide To Being a Witch because it feels like it just rehashes a lot of the concepts from here without adding much to it.

Not Alone - 3/10

Hooooo boy this one was a ride. My main criticisms-- the pacing is soooo slow sometimes. It drags so much in the beginning and towards the middle-end of the story. I think it took maybe 60 pages for... anything interesting to start happening? Don’t quote me on that. 

What you’ll heaps of in this book is tension. Some tension is good, but in combo with the slow pacing it became painful and I ended up skipping like 20-ish pages towards the end just to get to the final part of the story, and I don’t think I missed much (maybe going back to read those pages will change my mind, we’ll see.) 

Other (definitely stupider) issues... I wasn’t a big fan of the pov character (personal bias :P) and it took me quite a while to get on board with her... I think she’s just the type of person that I would never want to live inside the head of. At least when they FINALLY meet other characters it provides some contrast between her neurotic overprotective brain and the other calmer, kinder characters. The way the prose was written also kind of bothered me for the first few chapters (I can’t name any specific reasons... I did just learn to tune out the part of my brain that was bothered by it eventually. Maybe it was just overdescriptive?? Who knows).

ANYWAY. Despite the multitude of problems I had with this book, it still ended up being pretty emotional and compelling. The narrator’s experience feels very deeply emotionally charged and personal, I get the sense that the author relates very heavily to her character and gave her a lot of her own experiences. I don’t know jackshit about being a mother but the narrative drills into you how her neurotic and overprotectiveness of her child stems from her deep love for him. The end is depressing and bittersweet and made me ugly cry.

The casual misogyny and rapey-ness displayed by most of the male characters feels very disturbing but there are still several kind, compassionate male characters that helped restore some of my faith in humanity during my readthrough. 

	Okay sorry I had way too much to say and this review is All Over The Place so my apologies. 

The whole book is very serious and gritty and I definitely wouldn’t recommend it if you just want some lighthearted fun. If you’re willing to sit through a lot of depressing, somewhat repetitive writing you’ll find an emotional core that almost makes it worth it.

Pixels of You - 8/10

I honestly really liked this one. The focus of the story stays pretty narrow in scope as we explore the relationship between two girls. Enemies to friends to lovers but they're very sweet together. I'm not entirely sure how or if the story is meant to support the clearly anti-AI stance that is stated in small asides throughout the comic but I think the story works best if you don't take the robo girl's existence to be any sort of metaphor for real life AI. The focus of the story seems to be more on connecting with people that have completely different life experiences and coming to understand your common ground and accept those differences. 
The story can be a little confusing at first but it never felt frustratingly so, and a reread made a lot of things clearer to me (the photo show at the beginning and how it impacts the robo girl)
Kind of upsetting that it just ended with the kiss-- I wanted more! But that makes the most sense for the story I suppose.

Thief of the Heights - 5/10

An alright read. Goes pretty fast like most comics, the fast pacing is definitely to the detriment of the story in this case. The villains are cartoonishly evil in such an obvious way that undermines the weight of everything. If it had more time to flesh the society out and make it seem like the characters were even slightly tempted by a lavish ignorant lifestyle maybe the conflict would be more interesting and the societal commentary would be more compelling. I found the relationship between the main characters to be charming at least, even if I'm still somewhat confused on the whole overthrowing the government plan.

Five Kingdoms (Sky Raiders) - 6/10

The protagonist is your average obnoxious sixth grader. The characters and their relationships to each other aren't particularly compelling. However, the world building is genuinely fascinating and fun to contemplate. I enjoyed the various settings the characters found themselves in. The lore behind the bigger scheme with the king is delivered mainly through straightforward monologues which is not particularly interesting but it is still a good enough hook to maintain interests in further installations/the larger lore of the series.
