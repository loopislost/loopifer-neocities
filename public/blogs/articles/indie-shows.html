Intro

I've been deeply invested in the Indie Animation scene for most of my teenage years, and I've watched as public perception of indie projects has grown and shifted over the years and as certain projects have begun to hit the mainstream and have much broader cultural impact. I've seen a lot more indie shows (both animated and not) during my time on Youtube than the average person. Not sure if that makes my opinion any more worthwhile, but it does mean that I have opinions.

Indie Shows: A Brief (and probably incorrect) History

The reputation of Hazbin Hotel has tanked considerably since, but from my memory it was one of the first projects to put indie animation on the map. Not that there weren't other indie shows before this one, but something indie with the level of finish and animation quality of Hazbin was virtually unheard of. I was 12 years old when it first came out and heard about it but avoided watching it for at least a year or two (smart choice on my part). I'm sure plenty of criticism existed for the pilot back then, but I believe the culture forming around indie animation at that point was a lot more hopeful: Hazbin Hotel's pilot's unapologetic raunchiness was refreshing. Animation, a genre seen as being "for kids", now had a prominent very much not child friendly player on the board.

Of course, it was far from the first "adult" indie series to exist. I vaguely remember hearing about series like Salad Fingers being around long before. Indie shows had long been gory and unpleasantly adult, but they had remained fairly niche.

Helluva Boss, from the creators of Hazbin Hotel, began airing between the release of the pilot and the release of Hazbin Hotel (which had since been picked up by Amazon Prime). There was a lot of hype for it--and the discussion around it was a lot more positive while the first season was coming out! Of course, there people criticizing it back then, but the hatedom was nowhere near as big as it is now.

The Indie Animation Boom (+ the rise of GLITCH): In a time of mass dissatisfaction with Hollywood / "corporate" output, Indie Animation was looked up to as a beacon of hope for freedom for creators to make what they want, not whatever the corporate bigwigs approved. This started really picking up steam fairly recently, right around when the Amazing Digital Circus pilot came out, but I recall seeing similar discussion building up before then. I think the trigger for it was the end of several notable animated Disney Kids shows (The Owl House, and its lesser known cousin Amphibia). For the Owl House in particular, rumours that it had been prematurely axed by Disney due to its open queer representation (I have no idea how true those rumors may have been) and despite its massive popularity were rampant in the community. Fans were outraged at corporate censorship-- it was clear they had lost touch with what audiences wanted. The end of the Owl House left a gaping void that Disney couldn't manage to fill with any of their newer children's animated shows. On the other hand, quite a few noteworthy indie series were exploding in popularity, such as Murder Drones, Lackadaisy (only a pilot at the time), Ramshackle (also only a pilot), and others that I'm forgetting. Hazbin Hotel started releasing somewhere in this timeline (though that's arguably not super indie at this point, but it still shares a lot of characteristics with indie stuff)(basically no censorship basically) and that was insanely popular too.

A Golden Age?

The Amazing Digital Circus shocked everyone with how freaking massive it got after the pilot. I watched the pilot when it had maybe 300,000 views and thought it was decent but unimpressive. Image my surprise when everyone and their mother had watched it by the next morning (not really, I still don't think Millenials or Gen X'ers know much about it). If Hazbin Hotel had put Indie Animation on the map, The Amazing Digital Circus proved that indie animation could compete with the big corporations, without any help from those big corporations. It felt revolutionary.

Public perception (at least on Twitter (Not the best source, I know)) started souring more as TADC got more backlash. Video essays on TADC began to take a much more negative lean, as critics began to note the striking writing issues shared by a lot of these "indie" shows. The backlash to Hazbin Hotel and Helluva Boss has also been substantial throughout its life span. Despite the fact that indie shows were massively popular to audience and giving them things that big studios had failed to, they suffered from amateurish flaws and lack of polish. Could indie animation overcome its own flaws to be truly respected?

Present and Future of Indie Animation

TADC has finished airing its last episode, one which released in theaters throughout the Americas (and Japan? I remember there being controversy over showings in certain countries getting canceled.) Twitter, as always, was upset over it, but it proved that there was a big audience out there for indie animation, people that were willing to show up in theaters for it.

To fill the void, GLITCH already has several new properties on the way. At time of writing, Knights of Guinevere episode 2, run by Dana Terrace (creater of The Owl House) has released. From my perspective, it seems to be more professional and broadly well-liked among fans, even if not as explosively popular as TADC. GLITCH also has Gameoverse and The Gaslight District on the way, and picked up Lackadaisy too. Outside of Glitch, more niche shows like Monkey Wrench continue on and Spooky Month just released a new episodes (probably my favorite from that series). Calling All Villains - Pilot released a little over a week ago. I haven't seen it yet, but I've seen a bit of buzz around it online (and, funnily enough, it's a spinoff of SMG4, which is where GLITCH came from originally....All connected....). There's many more indie properties that have garnered their own fanbases that I could attempt to name Star vs The Forces of Evil, Ramshackle, Atlas and the Stars, etc.

Point is, Indie Animation has a bright future ahead of it. Even now the behemoth of the TADC is over, there's so many to look forward to and a thriving fanbase for many of these works.
