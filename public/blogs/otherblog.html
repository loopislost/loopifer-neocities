✒ 4/10 -- Arcane (Season 2) by Blank (Nov 1 2026)

I hold a personal grudge against Arcane Season 2 so it's difficult to be more... objective about it. I debated whether it made more sense to rate this season even lower, but ultimately I think a four is very fitting.

In a vacuum, Season 2 might be passable as an average show. The writing is very flawed, but it's still eye candy so I'll give it some credit for that. Or will I? WELL....to be honest, it infuriates me that so many talented artists wasted their time on a thoroughly subpar show that ruins the identities of its characters and discards everything that was interesting about its predecessor to deliver slop to the masses.

Season 2 is laughably bad. The first time I watched it, as it was coming out, it took me far too long to realize how shitty it was. I think it was into Act 2 when I started rolling my eyes at the idiotic writing. This time around I was able to analyze the first act of season 2 more critically. The first episode goes down pretty smoothly; it feels like a natural evolution from the end of season 2. As we get further into the season, it really starts to fall apart. Characters like Isha exist solely to manipulate the feelings of the audience and other characters, characters like Heimerdinger are reduced into babbling idiots stripped of the ideologies that made them so strong in the first season, and characters like Caitlyn or Viktor go through drastic changes that make them into entirely different characters. None of the interesting nuances of the first act are left intact; the story leans so heavily on malformed tropes.

The ending of this season feels deeply unsatisfying. Yes, they resolve character conflicts, but only the ones that they half-hazardly slapped together so they could handwave the actually interesting moral issues explored in the first season. Viktor and Jayce have a nice heartwarming "power of friendship!" moment that solves all the issues (apparently all Viktor needed was to be told he was beautiful the way he is!).

--SPOILERS--

I'm not sure how I feel about what they did with "The aRcAnE" this season. I feel like they were probably going to do something similar with it originally, but I feel like what they did with it this season hollows it out (especially the end). As a student going to an engineering school, we have a lot of discussions about ethics in science and how to safeguard the progress of technology. The Jayce & Viktor Hextech plotline from the first season really struck me a lot more this time around because it feels much more applicable to me now (and especially now with the Pandora's box of AI being opened...). In the second season, it kind of makes sense that the Arcane would then go on to destroy everything, but it feels much less like a parallel to anything grounded in the real world. The Arcane loses all of its potential to help people, because it being used at all means that it destroying everything is just a foregone conclusion. Maybe I wanted something more hopeful, especially knowing about the progression of technology in our reality.

The only REAL credit I feel obligated to give Season 2 is that I kind of loved episode 7. I admit this to be very self-indulgent as I clung to the only good (ish) thing they gave me in the shitshow that the rest of the season was. It's nostalgia-baiting me so hard and I'm falling for it. I think this episode works because it basically exists in a vacuum isolated from the rest of the series. It doesn't have to juggle complex plot threads or character motivations and it feels much more fantastical. It's like an episode from an entirely different show, and that gives it the room it needs to breathe. I really feel for Ekko here.

✒ 8/10 -- Arcane (Season 1) by Blank (Oct 20 2026)

FOR CLARITY: This review applies to Season 1 ONLY.

After my second time watching it, I can confirm, it's really good! If I hadn't seen Andor then this would be contender for the best show I've watched. The art style is fabulous (this was one of my big inspirations for learning Blender back in the day). I paid more attention to the environments this time around and they are also gorgeous. I don't like giving more than a passing mention to art/production quality in these, since in my humble opinion story should almost always take precedence. But this show's got everything.

My few criticisms are that all the slo-mo shots felt uber dramatic and goofy and some of the fight scenes are simply not that engaging to me. All the things that remind you it's a show made based on a videogame, in essence. It can also be kind of hard to follow the show if you're not paying close attention all the time (it cuts between different storylines very frequently). I think that was the main issue with my viewing experience the first time I watched it. Vi vs Sevika in the last episode is also puzzling to me because I'm not clear on what chain of events lead to them having to fight again, other than for cool factor.

✒ -- Hazbin Hotel S1E1 vs Pilot by Blank (Oct. 17, 2026)

This series has more dedicated haters than it warrants. I've spent too much time on Twitter and most of the insults people throw at the show and Vivienne Medrano are idiotic (it's Twitter... can you really be surprised?). Engaging with discourse about Hazbin or Helluva Boss is headache inducing at best, and I'm too cynical to be able to love either show. I do like some aspects of them! But there's a lot of actual, good faith criticism to be had.

I'm not going to assign ratings to either of these (I don't feel like doing so, and it would make more sense to rate it by season or just the whole series). I also have no desire to rewatch Hazbin Hotel currently. The only reason it's on my mind is because one of my friends coerced me into watching it with them (though they were thankfully dissuaded from watching the rest of the series).

We started out by watching the first episode of the actual series, and, well... It's not great. To be fair, S1E1 is easily one of the weakest of the series (from what I recall). It does have the redeeming quality of having a song that I genuinely enjoy (Hell is Forever) but otherwise it's generally uninteresting, few jokes actually land, and most egregiously, it answers the question of the premise of the show RIGHT off the bat.

What about the pilot? Well, it's not the most amazing thing I've seen, but I understand why it developed such a massive fanbase. Unlike the first episode, which is trying to speedrun everything, the pacing feels a lot more natural and the important characters get more time for a proper introduction. One thing that struck me the first time I rewatched the pilot after seeing the show is how much darker and hopeless the setting of Hell feels. Episode 1 waves around blood and gore and guts to try and persuade you that it's a horrible place but they maybe lean into the comedy too much? Meanwhile, the pilot feels a lot more grounded and Charlie stands out a lot more as the stupidly optimistic princess trying to change a broken world. From the pilot, you get the impression that everyone there (besides Vaggie and Charlie) are bad people who revel in their cruelty. You only get a very small hint that Angel Dust might want to be redeemed and isn't just leeching off the hotel's generosity. Alastor's cheerful mannerisms, on the other hand, contrast other denizens of Hell, but it makes him more unnerving than anything else.

From pilot to show-- what happened?

The pilot released in 2019, on Youtube. It would take 5 more years for the first episode of the actual show to air. Between pilot and show, it was picked up by Amazon Prime, we got an entirely new voicecast and small redesigns for all the characters. Visually, the actual show feels like a downgrade. Everything is cleaner and sharper but the colors are too saturated and painful and the animation feels stiffer. Musically, I sadly enjoy the show quite a bit. There are plenty of misses but the show would not have stuck around so well in my brain without the songs (ewwww musicals).

Character and plot wise? It's a huge disappointment. You can find plenty of youtube video essays going into more detail! Some of those videos might not even be grating to watch! But oooof the way they butchered show Alastor HURTS. Angel Dust is sometimes good in the show but suffers from flanderization and show Charlie is far more insufferable. The only character I can think of that improves is Husk (S1E4 is a highlight for me).

When you look at the show vs pilot, it's sad to see the lost potential. But especially after Helluva Boss season 2 aired, I don't think it would be fair for anyone to expect great or even good writing out of this show. We just need to accept that the Hazbin Pilot was a fluke and, while entertaining, expecting professional writing from Vivziepop shows only leads to disappointment.

A five might seem kind of harsh for this one, and it might be. It's a decent watch, has some modern day relevance. I watched it for school on YouTube at 360p so that might be affecting my enjoyment of it. It's hard to see more subtleties in the acting when they're all incredibly low quality.

✒ 9/10 -- Nerdy Prudes Must Die by StarKid (Sept 15 2026)

NPMD is campy in all the best ways. And I liked it better on rewatch?

All of the characters (except Ruth) have a compelling arc, Grace Chasity and Max Jagerman are superb (and for sure outshine Pete and Steph) but Pete and Steph are still really solid and they make a cute pair. And nearly all of the background characters are also likeable and pretty memorable?

And the music is bonkers, I loooove this one. "Nerdy Prudes Must Die" and "Literal Monster" are straight up bangers, "Dirty Girl" is ah... a song for sure, and I also liked most of the Pete & Steph duets. I know people love "Hatchettown" which I don't fully understand but its still good. Almost of the songs feel like they serve a purpose and rarely feel like they overstay their welcome.

Main criticisms: some of the song lyrics are perplexing. Just don't think about them too hard, I guess.

Also, all my praises of the songs have one exception: "Just for Once". This song isn't bad, but it isn't meaningful, doesn't advance the plot, and really should have been cut or replaced. It reads as a desperate plea to get you to like a joke character.

Oh and the whole Summoning thing with the Lords in Black is strange and a bit out of place but it's not a huge complaint from me.

While Twisted is objectively better story-wise, NPMD is a guilty pleasure. After the trainwreck that was Black Friday, this was such a pallete cleanser. It only lightly references the other Hatchetfield stuff, and it's a great standalone story. Watch it! This one's a gateway drug to the rest of StarKid.

✒ 2/10 -- Black Friday by StarKid (Sept 15 2026)

(This review is basically a vent, not sure how much other people will get out of this one. Feel free to skip)

I remember vaguely disliking this one when I first watched it, but when I tried to remember specific reasons why I would draw up blanks. I know this was one of my friend's favorite StarKid musicals though so I vaguely wanted to rewatch it and find out what I missed the first time around.

Now that I've finally gotten to rewatching it (5 or 6 years later?) I can confirm that this is absolutely horrible and I don't understand why anyone would like it, let alone state it's their favorite. (no offense to anyone that does like it. If you are one of these people, please reach out! I am genuinely curious to know what you see in it.) The musical is littered with atrocious writing decisions, unfunny jokes, and serious scenes that are funny because of how bad they are.

To be fair to it, I will now list all the things I DID enjoy about Black Friday.

1. Some of the singing is absolutely phenomenal. Dylan Saunders as Tom kills it. TGWDLM's singing can be rough at times but Black Friday is a pretty big step up.

2. I liked the whole Lex + Hannah + Ethan storyline. I found the song where Lex is about to die genuinely heartwrenching (until she gets Deus Ex Machina'd out of it). And watching Ethan and Hannah interact is actually cute. Although I forgot how abruptly Ethan dies in the musical.

3. The President going into the Black and White plotline (it was stupid but idc)

Thankfully I have solved the mystery of why I didn't remember anything from the first time watching it. Most of it is boring as hell with dreadful pacing so I definitely zoned out for most of it the first time. Good thing I was locked in THIS time so I know my dislike of it was justified!

Black Friday diverges from other StarKid musicals because it takes itself far more seriously than any of the other ones. Twisted and TGWDLM had fairly serious plotlines in them (Scheherezade in Twisted, Bill and his daughter in TGWDLM) but overall they were much more lighthearted. In Black Friday there are way more serious topics referenced throughout(the car crash, dealing with grief, abusive/neglectful parents and partners) and the violence feels way more graphic and (sort of) grounded. And then there's that one scene Becky talks about how she murdered her ex-husband and its meant to be a very serious moment? But it's so fucking corny? And it just makes me wonder what the hell they're doing (god Becky is an awful character). And I personally hate the Tom/Becky romance because of how hard it shoves it down your throat (and the introductory song to the relationship is so amateurish and high-school-y? I haaaate it)

Black Friday is ambitious, and it bites off way more than it can chew. It feels like it juggles a lot of different characters, most of whose arcs don't really intersect in meaningful ways. The most important plot threads I can think of are Lex+Hannah+Ethan, Tom+Becky, Linda (?) and President are all very disconnected from each other (the president one especially) and lack narrative parallels that might make them feel like they belong to each other. Most of the characters also do not feel like they develop. Tom is the only one that I can say definitively does, maybe Lex? but idk a lot of them are stuck as tropes.

My final question: How exactly did Black Friday end up being This Bad?

I checked and Nick & Matt Lang are cited as the writers on Wikipedia. They also wrote TGWDLM, Nerdy Prudes Must Die, and Ani, all of which I have seen and some of which I have loved. They were also involved with writing Every Single Other StarKid musical (except for the VHS Christmas Carols). So they have some excellent works under their belt. I have heard mixed things about a couple of other StarKid shows (like Firebringer) so I can only conclude that they have some impressive range in the quality of their work.

I would never recommend this unless you like torturing yourself. Please watch ANY of their other musicals. Thank you.
